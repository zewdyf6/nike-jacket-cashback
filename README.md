## The fabric name on the tag decides almost everything

Nike jackets share a silhouette language, so two jackets that look nearly identical on a product photo can behave very differently. Four fabric systems do most of the work:

- **Storm-FIT** — a breathable laminate that's windproof and waterproof. This is the one for rain and wind.
- **Therma-FIT** — insulation built to hold your body heat in cold weather. Usually paired with a water-repellent finish rather than full waterproofing.
- **Dri-FIT** — moisture-wicking knit fabric. Thinner, faster drying, designed for running and training rather than standing around in the cold.
- **ACG (All Conditions Gear)** — lightweight technical layering pieces built for outdoor use and stacking.

Add **Tech Fleece**, which isn't a weather technology at all but a two-layer fabric blend that's smooth on both sides, lighter than classic fleece and warmer than it looks. It's the reason a Tech Fleece Windrunner feels different from a Club Fleece hoodie even though both are, technically, fleece.

If you remember nothing else: Storm-FIT for rain, Therma-FIT for cold, Dri-FIT for sweat, Tech Fleece for the 50–65°F range where you want warmth without bulk.

## The five jacket types Nike actually sells

Nike's own buying guides and third-party reviews tend to sort the catalog into five buckets, and most models sit in one of them (some blur two):

- **Windbreaker** — thin woven shell, elastic at the waist, wrists and neck. The Windrunner sits here.
- **Puffer** — down or synthetic fill for real cold. The Therma-FIT Puffer line.
- **Rain jacket** — water-repellent, hooded, usually the lightest full-coverage option you'll find.
- **Track jacket** — breathable, moisture-wicking, casual-to-gym use.
- **Bomber** — leisurewear cut that gathers at the waistband.

Worth knowing: the Windrunner's chevron design lines trace back to a 1978 running windbreaker, not to a modern streetwear drop. That's why the shape looks familiar even on a jacket released this season.

## Fit and sizing, briefly

Nike outerwear runs on US sizing, and the model name usually tells you the cut. Anything labeled **Oversized** or **Loose** is cut generously — the Oversized Windrunner and the Loose UV Woven Windrunner are roomier through the body and sleeves on purpose, so don't size up out of habit. Tech Fleece Windrunner sizing runs XS to 4XL in the US, with tall variants in all sizes except XS. Windrunners and track jackets are typically bought true to size for a closer fit; puffers and Tech Fleece pieces get sized up when you plan to layer a hoodie underneath.

The practical advice: if you're between sizes on a puffer, the chest measurement matters more than the label, and returning a jacket that earned cashback will cost you that cashback.
